Camping options around Waynesburg and Greene County

Greene County is rolling farm country, and campgrounds here are spread out rather than clustered. Waynesburg itself is the county seat and has hotels, but no campground in town, so campers end up choosing between a state park to the west and the private campgrounds just over the West Virginia line to the south. What you need usually decides it for you.

  • Full hookups and year-round access. State Line Campground, about 25 minutes south in Blacksville, WV. Every site has 30 and 50 amp service, water, and sewer, and the campground stays open through the winter.
  • Rustic state-park camping. Ryerson Station State Park, west of Waynesburg, is the state-park option in Greene County and runs on a seasonal schedule. Good for a summer tent trip; check current dates and site types with the park before you go.
  • Long stays for work. Monthly sites near Waynesburg fill up with crews working the gas and energy fields. State Line offers monthly rates and is a common base for that kind of stay.

State Line is not the biggest campground in the region and does not try to be. There is no water park and no arcade. What you get is a quiet site with everything hooked up, and owners who live five minutes away and answer the phone themselves.

What every site includes

  • Full hookups with 30 and 50 amp electric service
  • Water, sewer, and waste disposal
  • Back-in sites that fit rigs up to 43 feet
  • Free WiFi at your site
  • Free laundry, with good washers and dryers, and no quarters needed
  • Free firewood, delivered right to your site
  • Clean bathhouse with showers and fire pits, plus picnic tables available on request
  • Drinking water on site
  • Pet friendly, with a dog walking service available
  • Daily, weekly, and monthly rates, with no hidden fees

Because electricity, water, sewage, waste disposal, and WiFi are all in the rate, the price you are quoted on the phone is the price you pay. See the full amenities list or how rates work.

Winter camping near Waynesburg

Most campgrounds in this part of Pennsylvania and West Virginia close for the season somewhere around late fall and reopen in spring. State Line does not. The campground is open 365 days a year, the hookups stay on, the bathhouse stays stocked, and the firewood keeps coming. If you are looking for a place to park in January, that is the difference worth knowing about.
